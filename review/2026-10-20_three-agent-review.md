## Bottom line

The content holds up; the packaging does not yet fit the room. All three reviews converge on the same three moves:

1. **Present 14 slides in about 24.5 minutes instead of 19 in 27.9.** That leaves about five minutes inside the 30 for
   interjections without losing Deliverables 3 and 4 off the end (R3, backed by R1's practice findings and the skill's Mode A).
2. **Lead Deliverable 2 with the capital question, not the opex split.** The brief describes "opposing views on capital
   prioritization"; the deck currently reframes that as a 70/30 opex split (assumption A4), which a panel can read as stepping around
   the hard part (R3).
3. **Strip the internal vocabulary.** "A6 valve", "T-16 lock", "gate-1", "INSTITUTIONALIZE", identical card labels and defensive
   footnotes are the strongest AI tells, and polished, generic language is what panels cite when they suspect AI-written material
   (R2, R1).

**One fact must be corrected before anything else.** The deck's "~$4.6B opex" on slide 4 is wrong. It was derived as efficiency
ratio times managerial revenue, but Nu's efficiency ratio is computed over net interest income plus fees, about $4.1B a quarter.
Reported Q2'26 operating expenses were $806.2M, so about $3.2B a year. The corrected figures are below; all are calculations and
labeled as such.

## R1 Research: what the evidence says

mid-level roles only: a consulting-style case round, a presentation, leadership interviews, about 37 days end to end, and one
manager-level report of a five-hour multi-person session (Glassdoor, anecdotal). Two Nu values are confirmed on Nu's own site:
"think and act like owners" and "pursue smart efficiency" (building.nubank.com).

**Leadership signals that are sourced.**
- Livingston's appointment release lists his remit (capital and liquidity, reporting, corporate development, tax, IR) and says a
  separate Brazil CFO role will report to him. Vélez's priorities: core-market growth, reshaping Nu around AI, disciplined
  international expansion (Business Wire, 6-K of 1 Jun 2026).
- **No public statement by Livingston on efficiency or test-and-learn was found.** The deck must not attribute a stance to him;
  v3 already removed the Capital One line, which is now confirmed as the right call.
- Q2'26: opex rose about 20% quarter on quarter, partly from **real-estate and marketing timing**; the efficiency ratio moved from
  17.6% to 19.5%; US entry is held to about 100 bps of efficiency-ratio impact while US credit data is built over 12-30 months;
  AI agents handle more than 60% of support chats in Brazil (Q2'26 call and release).

**Practice that is sourced or standard.** Lead with the answer and use assertion titles (Minto, sourced); engage every panel
member and stay calm on follow-ups (sourced); panels for Chief of Staff roles assess judgment, decision-making and comfort with
ambiguity (sourced); be ready to defend every number live, because firms are moving to conversational rounds to catch AI-assisted
answers (vendor survey, directional); keep **about 8-10 core slides for 30 minutes** with the rest in backup (general practice,
and the skill's own Mode A standard); end with the decisions you need, from whom, by when (general practice).

**Don'ts.** Generic, polished, buzzword-heavy content is the most cited tell of AI-written material (hiring-manager surveys,
directional); don't present estimates as Nu facts; don't describe the Q2 efficiency move without the timing nuance; don't read
slides; don't over-engineer.

### R1 second pass: new findings

- **Nu's values.** Search results list all five: customers love us fanatically; hungry and challenge the status quo; owners; strong
  and diverse teams; smart efficiency. The founders' letter uses "owners, not renters". Exact wording is confirmed for two only, so

  Expect directness.
- **Chief of Staff to the CFO, from job descriptions (not interview rubrics).** The role builds Board decks, runs the finance
  leadership agenda, ships Board materials in advance, and drafts communications to Board committees. No source frames the role as
  "honest broker or enforcer". General practice: show the trade-offs and let the CFO decide.
- **Livingston on the Q2'26 call (13 Aug 2026), per summaries.**
  - Risk-adjusted NIM was a record 12.4% and should stay in that region.
  - About a third of the beat came from Desenrola and two thirds from credit performance.
  - AI credit models are tracked "with the same discipline" as traditional models.
  - The US-entry cap of about 100 bps over 12-30 months comes from a call summary. Verify it in the full transcript before quoting.
  - No Livingston interviews, and no Visa or Capital One quotes on planning, were found.
- **Vélez.** "140 million customers with 10,000 employees" (summary). He gave up a variable-pay award to support the efficiency
  program (Nu newsroom). Both support a lean hub and are good spoken context.
- **Nu's real campus program (sourced, directly relevant to Pillar 3).**
  - More than R$2.5bn (about US$475M) of office investment over five years, announced January 2026.
  - Capote 210 in São Paulo (15,000 m², 1,300 workstations) opened in August 2026.
  - A 35,000 m² Pinheiros building is planned for 2027.
  - The January and August releases name different buildings, so caveat any detail.
- **AI-generated material (sourced, about applications and answers, not decks).**
  - 46% of 1,500 hiring managers have seen candidates use AI to answer questions (Resume Genius).
  - 67% of HR leaders say AI-generated applications slow hiring (Robert Half).
  - Managers are described as "anti-generic, not anti-AI" (secondary commentary).
  - No survey on AI-built decks was found.

### R2 second pass: spoken script, speaker notes, appendix, Q&A

**The spoken script.** Too many sentences run past what fits in one breath. R2 counted sentences over 30 words in each slide's
script. Worst: slide 2 (6 of 10), slide 12 (6 of 8), slide 7 (5 of 8), slides 8, 10, 16 and 18 (4 each). Cleanest: slides 5, 9, 11,
13 and 19. Slide 11, "Four moves, in the same order, every time", is the model for the rest. Typical tells:
- Openers such as "I want to be clear", "I want to be honest", "Here is the whole case".
- Spoken sums the chart already shows ("eight plus twelve plus fifteen plus twenty-five").
- Semicolon chains that cannot be said aloud (slide 17's stop rules).
- Day-by-day tours (slide 18). Name three dates and leave the rest on screen.

Sample rewrites:
- **Slide 1:** "Sixty days in, I see three problems. Sixty million of asks against a twenty-five million cap. A Board in four weeks,
  with two executives pulling opposite ways. And a regional HQ with no line authority for me."
- **Slide 7:** "Eight on the platform, twelve back for a case, fifteen if H1 beats plan, twenty-five funded." Replace "nothing is cut"
  with "every ask gets an answer".
- **Slide 17:** three short sentences, with "the stop rules" instead of "reversal triggers".

**Speaker notes (they travel with the file).**
- Slides 11 and 16 carry "[Pit wall: insert ...]".
- Slide 12 carries "Do not call this meeting the 'final statutory window'".
- Slide 17 carries "Context a short-seller would raise".
- Slide 3 carries "the brief asked for it and the CFO will test it", which shows the panel this is a coached exercise.
- All 19 presented slides use the same "Takeaway / Hardest question / Answer" template. It reads as machine-made, and the slide 12
  note runs to about 350 words.

**Appendix and footers.**
- "Scores are judgment (A9)" appears three times.
- The ledger title says "a directional benchmark that carries no weight". Better: "Every number is a sourced fact, a labeled
  assumption or context."
- Two footers over-explain: "Everything else on this slide is practice, not research" (slide 11) and "the first thing I would share
  in Q&A" (slide 16).

**Q&A sheet.** Answers 1-10 run 120 to 170 words, about 70 seconds each, and all open with "Slide N shows...". They sound memorized.
R2's model answer for Q2, at about 50 seconds:

- **Fit to the brief.** Deliverable 4 asks how you balance strategic influence with hands-on rigor; slide 16 answers a different
