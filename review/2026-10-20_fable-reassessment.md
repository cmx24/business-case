## 0. Bottom line

The candidate's claim is half right. D4 slide 14 is tactical where the brief asks for philosophy. D3 is not too tactical; it is labelled tactically. The brief asks you to "detail" the governance approach and names "key milestone gates", so gates with owners, exit tests and walk-aways are the answer, not a detour. What is missing is the sentence that says each gate is a decision the CFO makes, and the through-line that the same way of deciding runs all three pillars. That is a titling and framing fix on about six slides, not a rebuild. Verdict for the deck: MINOR REVISION. Do not add slides, do not drop specifics.

### The candidate's claim, head-on

**D3.** The brief's Pillar 3 challenge is: "How do you approach the governance, cross-functional execution, and risk management of this project while acting as an interim leader across non-finance verticals?" Deliverable 3 is: "Detail your governance approach for the regional hub/campus negotiation, highlighting key milestone gates, statutory officer implications, and cross-functional leadership." Three of the four nouns the brief uses are concrete: gates, officers, cross-functional leadership. "Detail" and "highlighting key milestone gates" are instructions to be specific. A D3 without dated gates, owners and walk-aways would score lower, not higher; my reframe this morning found v6's single-slide D3 "thin" for exactly that reason, and a panel with Legal or Tax on it would find the bottom of a philosophy-only answer in two questions.

So the feedback is right about one thing and costly on another. Right: the gates should be decision gates. Named by workstream (tax, grant terms, officers, lease), the listener hears four work packages; renamed as decisions (can we, what may we offer, who may bind Nu, do we commit), the same table becomes the way of working the challenge asks for. Costly: the feedback's five-gate sequence already maps onto the slide. Mandate is the charter signed today; feasibility is gate 1; negotiation authority is gate 2 plus the walk-aways, which are the red lines; binding commitment is gates 3 and 4; execution is the 90-day report. A fifth gate adds a slide and three minutes for no new content.

**D4.** Deliverable 4 is: "Briefly share how you balance high-level strategic influence with hands-on technical FP&A/governance rigor in a fast-paced, matrixed executive culture." That is a question about a way of working, and the candidate is right that slide 14 answers it with tactics. The three rules on slide 13 are the real answer and they stay, with the Citi, Itaú BBA and Visa opening. Slide 14's table should stop being "what I do with my own hands" and become "how the work is split in a matrix": what the Chief of Staff owns, what the experts own, what the accountable executive owns. Rows stay; columns and lead line change.

**Where the feedback would cost the candidate if followed literally.** (1) Its suggested title and thesis sentence ("enterprise operating system that makes trade-offs explicit...") are consulting voice; this deck's strength is that it sounds like an executive talking. Take the idea, not the wording. (2) Do not invent a scored "new bets first" option for the capital dispute. Name it in the memo; do not score it. (3) "Stop" must be honest: the deck says "neither executive hears a no", so the stop is the one already on slide 15 (two of four indicators below plan, the share is held; a case that fails the 60-day track is not funded). (4) A four-part strip on the first slide of every deliverable would cost an hour and crowd four slides; slide 3 already carries assumptions and "if wrong".

## 2. Minimum high-impact revision set, ranked

Applies in under an hour. Fifteen presented slides, no new slides, about 24 minutes.

**1. Slide 2 (highest value, ten minutes).**
Current title: "Three decisions today: the $25M plan, the Board calendar and the HQ charter".
New title: "One way of deciding, used three times: the plan, the Board, the HQ".
Add one strip under the title, above the four tiles: "The same mechanism runs all three. The rule is published before the argument. Options go to one named decision owner, on a date. The decision, the rationale and the dissent are recorded. Only exceptions come back up."
Keep the four tiles and THE ASK as they are. Why: this is the through-line the feedback asks for and the deck lacks; it also pre-answers "how do these three connect?", the first question a strong panel asks.

**2. Slide 11 (fifteen minutes).**
Current title: "Four gates in 120 days, each with an owner, an exit test and a walk-away".
New title: "Four decision gates in 120 days: the CFO decides, nothing is signed between them".
Gate labels in the table (column 1 only; keep every other cell): "Gate 1, D+30: can we? Tax and residence"; "Gate 2, D+60: what may we offer? Regulatory and grant terms"; "Gate 3, D+90: who may bind Nu? Officers and signing"; "Gate 4, D+120: do we commit? Lease and campus".
Replace the lead line "How I negotiate: Legal leads the room; I set the mandate with the CFO; nothing is signed outside a gate..." with: "Gate 0 is the charter signed today: why, the outcomes, the boundaries, the CFO as sponsor. Each gate after it is a decision the CFO makes on a pack I assemble; the walk-aways are the red lines of the negotiating mandate. Legal leads the room. What we give stays inside the published plan: no headcount or spend commitment the $25M cap cannot carry."
Why: turns four work packages into the mandate, feasibility, authority, commitment logic without losing the Pillar Two figures, 85 percent and 24 months, or the walk-aways.

**3. Slide 12 (five minutes).**
Current title: "Licensed executives sign; I run the pod, and the CFO breaks ties".
New title: "Experts own the judgment, I own the process, the CFO owns the residual risk".
In the "Leading as an interim across five functions" block, replace "Functions recommend; I assemble the gate pack and recommend; the CFO decides the gates" with: "I own the integrated outcome and the decision process; I do not second-guess a tax opinion. Each function owns its technical judgment. The CFO, as accountable executive, owns acceptance of any material residual risk at each gate; the CEO decides the site; the entity's board appoints officers; the Audit and Risk Committee hears at gates 1 and 3."
Why: this is the posture line the brief's "interim leader across non-finance verticals" is testing, and it answers "are you pretending to be the tax expert?" before it is asked.

**4. Slide 14 (ten minutes).**
Current title: "What I do myself and where I influence, problem by problem".
New title: "How I work in a matrix: what I own, what experts own, what I leave alone".
Column headers: "With my own hands" becomes "I own: the model, the memo, the log"; "Where I influence" becomes "I influence, with no line authority"; "What I leave to others" becomes "Others own: the decision, the signature". Rows stay. Replace the lead line "The team I build: ..." with: "The same split in all three problems. I own the process and the integrated recommendation. Experts own their technical judgment. The accountable executive owns the decision and its residual risk. The team I build: I hire for modeling and plain-English writing; each hub member owns one market's release pack; a named deputy sits in the HQ pod, so by the second release nothing depends on me alone."
Why: the candidate's own diagnosis; the evidence stays, the frame becomes philosophy.

**5. Slide 5 (ten minutes).**
Current title: "Every dollar of the $60M gets an answer: $25M funded in two releases, $15M held in reserve".
New title: "Five answers for $60M: fund now, stage, reallocate, defer or stop".
Add a category label to each bar or tile, no new numbers: $15M first release "fund now"; $10M second release "stage-gate on Q1 indicators"; $8M platform "fund through reallocation: the shared code base"; $12M case track "stage-gate: 60 days to a case, then fund, defer or stop"; $15M reserve "defer: conditional on H1 profit above plan". Add to the footer: "Stop is real: a unit below plan on two of four indicators has its second-release share held, and a case that fails the track is not funded (slide 15)."
Why: makes the decision categories explicit on the slide the CFO will stare at, reconciles $60M to $25M by category, and invents no BU allocation.

**6. Slide 7 (ten minutes).**
Current title: "The capital dispute gets one ranking: return on the equity each use needs".
New title: "The capital dispute becomes one choice for the CEO: four uses, one test".
Replace the footer "Where the two executives meet on opex: ..." with: "On opex, both positions go into the T-16 memo as options, core markets first, new bets first, or the staged 70/30 release. I recommend 70/30: mature cohorts earn about $31 a month at eight years against $17.1 for the group, and new bets are judged on activation, retention and unit cost, not first-quarter profit. The CEO decides; the dissent is recorded; the Board sees one recommendation. If the dispute is about equity, the test above decides it (A4)."
Why: the title no longer sounds like the Chief of Staff settling a peer dispute; the mechanism in the body is unchanged. Do not score the "new bets first" option in backup C; it is named, not modeled.

**7. Slide 15 (two minutes).**
Keep the title. Add one sentence to THE ASK: "You are approving one way of deciding, used three times, and I report on it in 90 days." Why: closes the loop opened on slide 2.

Optional, if time remains: one footer line on slide 10, "What would change this answer: if a site shortlist already exists (A13), the four hard tests run on it at gate 1 and the five criteria become the tie-break." Slides 4 and 7 already say what would change them.
